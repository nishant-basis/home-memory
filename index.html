BEFORE HELLO · THE FIRST LOOK

The First Look

I had just walked into the venue. Most of the faces around me were familiar — common friends, people from the team, the usual crowd.

And then, somewhere in the middle of all those familiar faces, I noticed you.

A beautiful face. Those big, bright eyes. And strangely, there was something nostalgic about it.

“I know her from somewhere.”

The funny part? I didn't. Your face reminded me of someone I knew, but my brain had conveniently decided not to tell me who.

I should probably have just said hello. ♡

THE QUESTION · 7 MARCH

The Very Important Question

By this point, one thing had become clear. I wanted to know who you were.

A sensible person would have walked over, introduced himself, and simply asked.

Apparently, sensible was not the route I had chosen that evening. So I asked your friend which team you were from.

You were standing right there.

Looking back, that was probably my first attempt at finding a way into your story without actually having the courage to start it directly.

Smooth move, Nishant. ♡

When I Miss You

Not every thought needs a particular date, occasion or reason. Some simply arrive — and deserve somewhere to stay.

When I miss you, the first thing that comes to mind isn't some big, cinematic memory.

It's the little things.

Your smile in the middle of an ordinary moment.
That specific tone in your voice when you're teasing me.
Those lighthearted taunts that somehow always catch me off guard.

It's realizing how strangely quiet a routine day can feel without the spontaneous, silly fun we usually share.

The inside jokes.
The banter over absolutely nothing.
The effortless comfort of simply having you around.

I think that's what missing you really starts with.

Not one particular moment.

Just all those tiny things that became so familiar that I stopped noticing how much they meant.

Because somewhere along the way, those little things became home.

You will probably never fully understand what you mean to me — or all the ways you've changed me as a human.

Because when I'm with you, I feel like the real me.

The version without the filters.
The version that doesn't have to be serious all the time.
The version whose inner child somehow gets permission to come out and play.

Sometimes I honestly feel like I'm the girlfriend in this relationship. 😂

The way you handle my tantrums.
The way you somehow always make me feel special.
The way you feed me with your own hands.

The little things that probably don't even register as big moments to you.

Like sitting beside each other at a restaurant instead of across the table — just so you can lean over my shoulder.

Or the way you keep updating me about the smallest, most random things happening in your day.

None of these things would make sense as some grand romantic gesture.

But that's exactly the point.

Somewhere between the teasing, the tantrums, the little updates, the food you make me eat, and the shoulder you lean on...

I became a version of myself that I didn't know was still there.

And maybe that's one of the biggest things you've given me.

You made me feel completely comfortable being me.

You don't always say that you care about me.

You just... do things.

You make me eat when I would happily keep talking and forget about food.

You notice the little things.

You keep updating me about the smallest things happening around you — something from work, something your friend said, some random story you just discovered — because somehow I always become the person you want to tell first.

You even change what you eat sometimes because I don't eat spicy food.

And then, once in a while, you look at something with that "tadak bhadak chahiye" expression and go back to your Kurkure anyway. 😂

You scold me when I waste money.

You find coupons when we go out and somehow turn my so-called "high maintenance girl" into the person making sure we don't pay more than we need to.

My ambitions.
My decisions.
The things you think I should be doing better.

Sometimes it feels less like you're giving advice and more like you're my very own personal management department. 😂

But underneath all of that is something I've come to understand.

You care deeply.

About me.
About your family.
About the people you love.
About the things that matter to you.

You may have your tantrums.
You may get upset when you don't feel prioritized.
You may sometimes feel like nobody understands you.

But give you a little love and attention... and somehow you multiply it and give it all back.

That's the thing about you.

You always say:

"I have loved you the most."

And you know what?

I believe you.

Because honestly, no one has ever loved me the way you do.

You were also the one who taught me something I don't think I'll forget:

There is no "love you." There is only "I love you."

Because love isn't something that simply exists between two people. It's something one person chooses to give to the other.

And you're also the one who keeps reminding me that when it's us, there isn't always a right person and a wrong person.

There is just us.

I don't think you realise how deeply this particular thought has stayed with me.

When you say:

"Tum sahi galat karte reh jaoge... aur sab khatm ho jayega."

It hits me every single time.

Because you're right.

I can keep proving that I'm right.
You can keep proving that you're right.

We can keep counting who said what, who did what, who was wrong and who was justified.

And somewhere in all that...

sab khatm ho jayega.

Sometimes the relationship matters more than winning the argument.

Sometimes saying "I'm right" isn't worth losing "us."

And maybe that's another way you love me.

You don't just love the person I am.
You keep reminding me to become a better person for the person I love.

So yes...

You always say you've loved me the most.

I accept.

No one has ever loved me quite the way you do.

And maybe that's why I've learned to notice the little things too.

The food you make me eat.
The random updates.
The scolding.

that's how you love.

The Annual Tradition

Goa had quietly become something of our own.

After the first trip, we decided that we should come back at least once every year. August is my birthday, September is yours, but the actual dates belong with our families — so, naturally, we invented our own date somewhere in the middle. 😂

And that's how Goa became our little birthday tradition.

We weren't celebrating on a date that mattered to the calendar.

We were celebrating on a date that mattered to us.

This Time, You Planned

After our first Goa trip, we had learned something important:

We were probably better planners than MMT. 😂

And because you already knew that I would look at every hotel, restaurant and plan through one very important filter — "how much?" — you took the planning into your own hands this time.

You planned the trip using everything we had learned from Goa I.

I mostly just participated, checked prices and pretended I was helping.

And honestly, I loved seeing you take charge of our little escape.

North Goa, Again

This time we kept things simple.

It was a short trip, so we stayed in North Goa at Holitel — close to the beach and genuinely wonderful.

We went around Calangute and Vagator, found our way back to places that already felt familiar, and let Goa be Goa.

There was no pressure to cover everything. No giant checklist.

Just us, the beach, the heat, the chaos and a few places that had slowly become part of our own Goa map.

The Goa That Came With Work

This Goa came with something our first one didn't: work stress.

It was hot. Chaotic. Some of our old favourite places weren't serving food like they used to. And Europe was waiting right after the trip.

Passport submissions. Forex card arrangements. MO raising. Interview sections. Last-minute information. Incomplete information. And a project lead who somehow managed to make everything more complicated.

You were carrying so much in your head that eventually it started spilling into us.

So yes, we fought.

Then we sorted it.

Because apparently that's another one of our traditions.

We Can Fight About Anything

There is genuinely no subject too small for us to fight about.

One of my personal favourites from this trip was the document that was at your house and you asked your brother to scan.

Does that make any sense as a reason to fight?

Absolutely not.

Did we somehow turn it into a fight anyway?

Obviously. 😂

That's us.

We can take the smallest, most meaningless thing in the world and turn it into a full argument.

And then, somehow, we're completely fine again.

The Pasta Peace Treaty

We had fought.

So naturally, we went to our pasta place.

By now we were regular enough that the waiters had started recognizing us.

Another couple sitting opposite us apparently noticed the fight too. We were ordering separate things, sitting there with our serious faces, making it very clear that peace negotiations were nowhere close.

Then the food arrived.

A forkful of pasta came towards me.

I refused.

You scolded me.

I ate it.

And just like that, the war was over.

One second we were fighting. The next, we were being romantic again while the older couple across from us laughed at the entire performance. 😂

Apparently, pasta is our version of couples therapy.

Your Aura

This was one of those completely random moments that should mean absolutely nothing — and somehow became a memory.

We went into a shop together to buy something. I stepped outside to get the car.

The shopkeeper looked at you and asked:

"Madam, stuff chahiye kya?"

We both burst out laughing.

Because apparently your aura says, "I definitely know where to get the stuff."

And me? Apparently I look like a milk-drinking innocent boy. 😂

It was completely stupid.

Which is probably why we remember it.

The Airport Fight

Even the ending had to be dramatic.

We started fighting at the airport.

You threw your boarding pass. You hadn't eaten. My sugar was dropping on the plane.

Basically, the grand finale was going exactly as expected.

And then, while still completely angry, you put chocolate into my mouth.

Not because the fight was over. Not because you had suddenly become romantic. Just because, even while angry with me, you still knew I needed to eat.

Honestly, nothing could have been more romantic at that moment.

And by the time the plane landed...

we were love birds again. ❤️

That's probably the best way to describe us.

Chaos first. Love immediately after.

PARIS · FROM FAR AWAY

Paris, From Far Away

Paris was supposed to be your trip.

Amsterdam, weekends in Belgium, Paris, everything happening one after another.

But somehow, I felt like I was travelling with you.

You kept telling me everything — where you went, what you saw, what happened, what you bought. Every little thing.

It felt like I was getting a running commentary of your entire trip, as if distance had somehow forgotten to exist between us.

And then came the gifts. Chocolates from everywhere. Something for everyone. Little things you saw and thought someone would like.

Because, of course, that's you. Even while travelling, you were thinking about everyone else.

But somewhere in all those stories, Paris stayed with me differently.

The Eiffel Tower.

That was the moment I thought...

I should be there with you.

Not watching it through your pictures. Not hearing about it through your stories. Standing there beside you.

And I remember thinking that I don't want this to remain one of those places you saw and I heard about.

I promise we'll visit the Eiffel Tower again.

But next time, we'll be standing there together.

And honestly, knowing everything you were juggling at the time makes me admire you even more.

Last-minute shopping. Clothes. Packing. Work pressure. A thousand things happening at once.

And somehow, you still managed all of it.

You really are my lioness. 🦁❤️

JIM CORBETT · THE TRIP YOU DON'T COUNT

The Trip You Don't Count

We wanted a road trip.

We thought about Ranthambore. Gir. And then, somehow, we landed on Jim Corbett.

Partly because I remembered it. And partly because I already knew how much you love wildlife.

So we packed up and drove.

Six hours of road, conversations, little halts, random stops and that feeling that we were actually getting away for a while.

And then we reached the hotel.

Big room. Beautiful pool. Live music. Everything we could have asked for.

We were genuinely so happy with our choice.

And then night happened. 😂

The shampoo bottle fell.

We ignored it.

Then it happened again the next night.

And suddenly, we had a very serious paranormal investigation happening in our room.

You were terrified. I tried everything to convince you that nothing was happening.

But that's not even what I remember most.

I remember the safari. The excitement. The odomas. The bees that gave us the most unexpected welcome.

No tiger.

But enough deer calls and jungle sounds to keep us entertained — and, of course, enough material for us to make fun of everything that happened. 😂

It was short. It wasn't perfect. It probably won't even make your official list of trips because apparently this one "doesn't count." 😂

But I remember it.

And sometimes that's enough.
